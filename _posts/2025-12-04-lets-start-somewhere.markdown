I used to be a writer. I wrote fiction for fun, I had a blog, I did a Creative Writing course focused on writing a first draft for a novel at the age of 16. I wanted to become an author. I kept several blogs through the years: poetry, short stories, silly paragraphs. I was a professional writer for a bit, too; I'm the author of a few interior design books, as random as that is. While in London, I joined a writing group, to which I went religiously for an entire year, and then sporadically after that because I got a boyfriend who, by the way, was completely supportive of my writing endeavour, so this not at all his fault. But life was busy, so I stopped going to that writing group, and eventually I stopped writing all together.

I admire great writers, but even mediocre writers get my appreciation because, no matter how bad their novel is, at least they wrote one. And *finished* it. Which is not something I can say about myself, so kudos to them.

Then I became a Product Designer and I was writing internal documentation and presentation decks, which is not exactly Nobel Prize worthy stuff, but my team appreciated how easy (and even engaging) it was to read, so in a way I was still writing. And then ChatGPT happened to me (well, to all of us, really) and I stopped writing all together. Until now.

In the last few months I've been trying to write more, because writing is thinking. I have a notebook where I write a review of every book I read, and it's a way for me to clarify my thinking. I don't publish those reviews anywhere, by the way – the point is for me to feel free to write whatever is true, not whatever reads well. To think through writing. My hope is that eventually my writing skills will improve as well, as will my thinking process.

I was on LinkedIn the other day and, reading post after post, I realised they all have the same structure. They even have the same format. I go on someone's blog post and I can tell they didn't write it themselves. Sure, maybe they tweaked the final version, but AI had a big influence on the text. And I've decided I don't want that for this blog. This blog needs to feel 100% mine, warts and all.

This is not a critique on anyone else's online presence, by the way. I know I use ChatGPT way too much, to an embarrassing degree. And the copy on my portfolio has definitely not been written by me. But this is. Everything on this site should be. 

I encourage you to do the same.
