I find it hard to be truthful online. Not that I lie either – I just don't say much these days. 

I've been looking for a new role for over a year. And this is not about me whining; this is about my actual experience and the experience of many others in my position: it's really, truly very hard. I know, that's three words that don't mean much, and yet I hope they serve to emphasise how much this sucks. 

You go into an interview, and you make a huge effort to bring your best self forward in the hopes that the other person will select you out of a pool of perfectly decent candidates like yourself. They tell you who they are, and you tell them who you are and what you've done and how your latest work experience ended a year ago. And then, they ask you: so, what *have* you been doing? Like, with *all* this free time? And you try to talk about how you've been up-skilling yourself and learning to use *Cursor* and taking Figma courses, and all of that is kind of true, but it's not the whole truth. 

Because the truth is I've been learning how to get a job, and I clearly haven't mastered it yet, so there's that. But being a job seeker these days is the hardest job ever. It requires research skills and editing skills, I have been recording videos and merging clips as well, so I had to actually download and learn to use video-editing software. For a job application. For a product designer role. I've learned the ins-and-outs of every single company that has invited me to interview, spending hours researching what they do, their product suite, the space they are in, their employees and where they've worked in the past. I've prepared answers to so many questions that have never been asked to me, only to be asked some stuff that I never thought of and be judged by that. I've prepped case studies and been asked scenario-based questions, and prepped scenario-based questions and been asked to show my Figma files, and I just never know what I'm going into anymore. Interviews used to be sort of similar and you knew what to expect, but not anymore.

And yet there's another truth that is harder to disclose: I've been very upset. I've cried a lot. I've felt like giving up quite often. And if you really believe being unemployed for so long shouldn't affect a person's ability to go on applying and interviewing and getting rejected, you must not be a human being at all. It's impossible not to let it get to you. How on Earth can it not impact our ability to prepare, interview for and secure a job? How do you expect it not to impact our wellbeing, our eagerness to learn and keep trying? We're people. We're not robots.

And what no one seems to value is the amount of resilience being in this position requires. Strength. Tenacity. Hopefulness. Energy. Enthusiasm. Resourcefulness. Humility. I'm sorry, are these not qualities you're looking for in your next employee?
