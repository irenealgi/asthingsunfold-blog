Yesterday I built a job application tracker in a few hours. It's not finalised yet, mostly because you can't sign up or log in, and I'm not confident it's capable of storing long-term data, which is necessary for an app like this. It currently lives in *Lovable*, but my plan is to put it in production using *Codex* and essentially build a standalone product. I'm not sure where I'm hosting it yet, but I'll figure that one out. I believe I can figure all of this out because I have AI on my side and I'm married to a Software Engineer. But that's not the point of this post.

The point of this post is that I built a thing in a few hours, and it works fine. *It works fine!* Once I sort out the technicalities, it will do the job. But it's dead inside. It doesn't have a soul. Because it was built by a freaking robot.

![enter image description here](https://res.cloudinary.com/dt0vjleqy/image/upload/v1770893621/Screenshot_2026-02-12_at_11.42.22_x71gf9.png)

I'm reading *Four Thousand Weeks: Time Management for Mortals* by Oliver Burkeman, which addresses the problem with productivity and this need to do everything *faster* so that we can do *more*. And you may have noticed, if you've read previous posts, that this is something I'm currently struggling with.

I'm on the chapter on patience right now, and it's so eery the way Burkeman talks about the microwave and how we can't wait for the whole minute sometimes, to the point that people stop it *eight seconds* before it finishes. Now, I don't do *that*, but I do find myself feeling nervous when stuff takes just a bit longer than I want it to. It's so weird how fast we get used to things going faster.

Two years ago, building that thing in the screenshot would have taken us (a group of technical people) a month to build, and I'm being optimistic. Because it would have taken so much more effort and time, we would have spend a good chunk of time discussing the product, the functionalities, the trade-offs, maybe we would have done some research (!) to find it out of this was worth building in the first place. And then, once the designs were approved, engineering would need to build it.

But now I can do this by myself and in record time, so the question that follows up for me is – so... now what? Because that thing works and is *fine*, but literally anyone else can build it in the same amount of time, so why on Earth would anyone use (and pay) for mine. So now we need to be *better*. We need to do *more*. Not less! More. With more.

Because the amount of players in the AI space is truly overwhelming, and the amount of new features and functionalities they all come out with every ten seconds is also overwhelming. Am I alone in this? Am I the only one who sometimes wants the train to stop so that I can get off? Hide in the mountains and take care of some sheep or something?

I was so pleased with my little thing that I built in two hours and then I realised it felt like frozen pizza. It does the trick, it's tasty enough and it fills up your stomach, but it's bland, and artificial, and extremely forgettable. It's just not satisfying.

Here is my plan, because I always need a plan. My plan is to add a soul to the soulless thing I built. To give the Creature a reason to live instead of just life. But the stakes are higher than ever. Designing and creating are not getting any easier. Designing and building things that work is no longer enough. I know some people will find this exhilarating, and it is, but part of me finds it exhausting. Because we think AI is going to make our lives easier, but I get the impression that life is only getting more demanding, as we also are.

I don't know. Maybe I'm just getting old.
